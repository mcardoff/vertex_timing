Timing selection across samples

The Z+jets method, run in-domain on every μ = 200 sample and the three μ = 0 controls. It improves every sample — from +4.14 on dijet to +7.92 on Z+jets — and stays quiet on the controls, where there is nothing to win. One caveat matters more than the numbers: the method emits a time, not a cluster, so the familiar oracle is no longer its ceiling.

Three things generalise cleanly across every topology:

  • Learned selection beats the hand score by 3.0–5.9 points before any re-timing (row 2 vs row 1). Largest on Z+jets, as expected.
  • Re-timing the chosen cluster is free money — +0.9 to +1.7 everywhere (row 3 vs row 2). Reweighting a cluster's tracks by the per-track hard-scatter tagger and Winsorising twice improves the time without touching which cluster was picked.
  • The end-to-end t₀ beats its formula counterpart on all four (+0.6 to +1.2), and is a genuinely different answer — the union sits 1–3 points above either alone.
The meta captures roughly a third of the union spread

Union minus base is 0.9 (vbf), 3.4 (zjets), 1.5 (dijet), 1.3 (ttbar); the meta converts 0.27 / 0.36 / 0.24 / 0.28 of that. Choosing between two good answers remains the weakest link, and it is weakest precisely where the spread is largest.

On ceilings

The number this study quoted for two years is no longer the right bound.

The familiar reference — best cluster by |Δt|, giving 98.3 / 92.6 / 97.9 / 98.0 — assumes the answer is a cluster. This method emits a time built from tracks, so it is not bounded by the clustering at all. Two consequences, both worth stating plainly:

perfect-tagger aggregate97.0282.3595.6096.16the correct reference
The method scores above the cluster-selection ceiling on every sample

Restricted to clusters that actually carry hard-scatter content, the best available cluster reaches 51.15% on Z+jets — and this method reaches 69.79%. That is not a contradiction: it is direct evidence that the answer is no longer coming from a cluster. Any future comparison that quotes the best-cluster oracle as “the ceiling” is comparing against the wrong quantity.

Against the correct reference, the fraction of available headroom actually captured is 73% (vbf), 51% (ttbar), 47% (dijet), 39% (zjets). Z+jets remains both the biggest absolute gain and the largest remaining gap — 12.6 points of reachable headroom still on the table.

Caveats

Things that would change a reader's confidence, stated rather than buried.

Z+jets reproduces at 69.79 here, not the shipped 70.00

Two deliberate differences, neither tuned away. This pooled meta includes ttbar (the shipped configuration pooled zjets+vbf+dijet only), and the end-to-end t₀ came in at 68.72 against 69.01 on a different fold-seed draw. The base answer reproduces exactly at 69.43 — the deterministic part, given the folds — which localises the 0.21 to meta composition and torch seed variance.

Read 69.8–70.0 as the honest interval for this configuration, not 70.00 as a fixed point.

  • The threshold plateau is flat, which is reassuring: 0.40–0.55 spans 69.69–69.79 on Z+jets and 95.22–95.26 on vbf. No cliff-edge tuning.
  • Truth is used in three places and none of them at inference: the per-track tagger label (truth_is_hs), the stage-1 and meta labels (the 60 ps criterion), and evaluation. A deployed version needs the tagger trained once on MC and frozen — that transfer is not measured here.
  • Per-sample in-domain training. Each sample gets its own tagger and stage-1; only the meta is pooled. The earlier frozen-transfer study showed a VBF-trained guard transfers positively to every sample, but this stronger pipeline has not been transfer-tested.
  • Z+jets uses novbs augmentation (94k events, folds hashed on the event key so a test event cannot leak through its novbs copy). The other samples train on canonical only.
  • Statistical scale differs by 100× across samples: zeejets_mu0 has 4,524 events, so its +0.05 is two events. vbf and ttbar at ~0.5M are the tightest numbers here.

What I would do next

  • Freeze-and-transfer the tagger. The single largest gap between this and something deployable. Train the per-track tagger on one sample, freeze, and re-measure everywhere — the same experiment that validated the guarded metric.
  • Attack the meta, not the answers. Two-thirds of the union spread goes uncaptured, and on Z+jets that is 2.2 points sitting between two answers we already compute.
  • Re-baseline the group's ceiling language. The best-cluster oracle is quoted throughout the existing notes; for any time-producing method it overstates the target by 10–40 points depending on sample.
