The topology cut that wasn’t

Our VBS selection required |Δη| ≥ 3 on the candidate jet pair. Measured against truth, that cut is purity-neutral on VBF and purity-negative on Z+jets and dijet — on two samples out of three it admitted more pileup-legged pairs than it removed. Swapping in mjj is at least as good everywhere.

What was actually being asked

Method: one denominator, both variables, truth as the referee.

A “VBS topology” requirement is supposed to select events whose forward jet pair comes from the hard scatter. At μ = 200 a jet pair can just as easily be assembled out of pileup, and nothing in |Δη| or mjj forces the legs to be real.

So we defined a pair as genuine when both legs are truth-HS-matched, and scanned both variables over the identical denominator: every event the rest of the selection admits, with an opposite-hemisphere pair, and no topology cut applied. Two numbers per working point — purity (genuine / kept) and efficiency (genuine kept / all genuine).

The two variables are not equivalent

VBF, 112,400 events, 48,640 passing everything except the topology cut. Baseline purity 57.11%.

Each point is a cut value. Up is purer, right is more efficient. The dashed line is applying no cut at all — a variable is only doing something if it lifts the curve above it.

|Δη| never leaves the baseline

Across its whole usable range the |Δη| curve sits on the no-cut line: 57.11% → a peak of 57.28%, then it turns over and drops below baseline past 4. It is discarding events without enriching what remains. mjj reaches 61.99%, nearly 5 points above baseline.

On two samples out of three the cut was actively harmful

dijet loses 5.1 points of purity to a cut whose stated purpose is to raise it. The mechanism is the one we should have expected: a genuine forward hard-scatter pair is rare, so requiring a wide rapidity gap preferentially selects the events where a pileup jet supplies the second leg.

What mjj does not fix

Z+jets has no forward VBS topology to select for

Its baseline purity is 2.69%, and mjj moves it by +0.2. Out of 2M events processed there are 1,872 genuine pairs. Neither variable can enrich a population that is essentially not there, and no reco-jet kinematic will — only truth matching separates these, which is not available at selection time.

This matters beyond the cut itself: it is the same fact that makes Z+jets the hardest sample for the timing analysis. The forward jets it selects on are overwhelmingly pileup-supplied, which is exactly the regime where a jet-proximity score has nothing real to weight toward.

Neither variable is monotonic

Both turn over. mjj peaks near 600–800 GeV and degrades above ~2 TeV, where a single far-forward pileup jet manufactures a large invariant mass. |Δη| degrades past 4–5 the same way. A tighter cut is not automatically a purer one.

Why the orange band inverts between the samples

The category names carry a verdict the numbers do not support.

“HGTD can help” means one specific configuration: a forward pileup jet with no hard-scatter jet anywhere in the event. VBF is a two-jet signal and essentially always has hard-scatter jets, so the case barely occurs — 391 events out of 634,565. Z+jets with Z→ℓℓ frequently has no hard jet at all, so any forward jet above 30 GeV is a fake.

The categories describe the event, not the benefit

A large orange band in a background sample says “this background is largely fakes, which timing can attack”. That is the detector working as intended, and it does not compete with VBF. Reading it as “HGTD does more for Z+jets than for signal” is the wrong comparison.

The clearest evidence that the labels mislead is VBF’s 73.31% in “may not help”. That row is dominated by genuine forward tag with no competing fake (72.09%) — an unambiguous signal event with nothing to disambiguate. The detector is not failing there; there is simply no fake to reject. Lumping that together with “no forward jet at all” (1.13%) hides the distinction that matters.

What changed

The default is now no |Δη| requirement, mjj ≥ 200 GeV. Both remain runtime flags (--vbs-deta=, --vbs-mjj=), and every output file is tagged with its selection so a re-cut run cannot overwrite a standard one.

200 GeV is deliberately conservative

It is not the purity optimum — that sits nearer 600–800 GeV. It is set low so the new baseline costs almost no efficiency (99.78% of genuine pairs survive) while removing a cut that was measurably harmful. Studies wanting the purer working point ask for it with a flag rather than a rebuild.

Honest framing for the group: this is the better of two imperfect proxies, not a solution. Neither variable enforces that the candidate pair is hard-scatter; only truth matching does that, and it is not available at selection time.
