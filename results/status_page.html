The 25-point gap

A learned selector beats both hand-designed scores on every sample, including one it had never seen. On Z+jets it reaches 67.1% against a 92.6% ceiling. Half that gap is unreachable by any selector. Of the reachable half, four candidate explanations have now been closed by measurement — including a jet tagger whose perfect version scores 15 points worse. What was left was a column of the ntuple nobody had read.

The oracle is P(∃ cluster with |t − t_truth| < 60 ps) — whether a good candidate exists at all. So the 7.4% beyond it is unrecoverable by any selector, and the 25.5 points between 67.1 and 92.6 are pure selection failure, with candidate availability already excluded.

This matters for how the gap is read. A recurring worry is that much of it might be events where no good cluster exists — that the problem isn't really cluster selection. It isn't: that population is 7.4%, and it already sits outside the 25.5.

And the 25.5 splits almost exactly in half

Inserting a rung for a perfect per-track HS tagger — sum pT over the tracks that genuinely came from the hard-scatter vertex, pooled by the current rule, with real measured times — separates the two halves. It needs no model: it is what a flawless tagger would hand the existing machinery.

The split, on Z+jets

+13.0 — track identity. A perfect per-track HS tagger takes 66.4 → 79.4. Reachable in principle by a better selector.
+13.2 — time quality. Perfect track identity still cannot reach the oracle. No selector closes this, however good: the times themselves are the limit.

The earlier round's 80.2% figure re-derives to 79.4% at the canonical selection, so that decomposition survives the fiducial change. Restricting the perfect tagger to tracks that actually carry a time changes nothing on any sample, so untimed hard-scatter tracks are not what is costing us.

Hypothesis rejected

A natural mechanism is that failures cluster where two candidates sit close in z, their times overlapping within resolution. They do not. Z+jets efficiency against distance to the nearest other candidate is flat from below 0.2 mm to above 5 mm: 66.2 / 65.9 / 66.1 / 68.3 / 66.1 / 63.7. Candidate crowding in z is not the mechanism.

The one thing that improved selection

A defect, not a tuning knob. Same seed, same config, same machine.

Validation core fraction was unreliable: on VBF it collapsed to 17.6% and degraded with training while the same weights scored 91.7% on test. The slice is sound — its oracle matches test's to 0.2 points, and TRKPTZ pushed through the identical tensor scores 90.0%. The fault is in the argmax-of-model-scores step on that slice, and it was selecting epoch 1.

Validation loss is the training objective itself, reads the whole score distribution rather than only its argmax, and moves smoothly — so it sidesteps the failure rather than depending on it. Core fraction is still reported every epoch, because the two disagreeing is the signature of the open bug.

Still open

The root cause is not understood. Nothing depends on the broken signal any more, and a control column now pushes TRKPTZ through every tensor so a recurrence is visible immediately — but this is a symptom fix, not a cure.

Four things that did not work — and why that is the finding

Each was a genuine attempt with a stated hypothesis. All four failing in the same direction is what makes the conclusion strong.

  • More Z+jets data: null. measured Over a 5.5× range (4,688 → 25,589 events actually fitted) core fraction moved 0.2 points inside a ±0.8 band. Z+jets is information-starved, not statistics-starved.
  • Seed ensembling: null. measured Four members gave −0.0 / +0.1 / +0.0 / +0.1 against the best single member.
  • Model capacity, pooling form, auxiliary head, feature sets: null. prior All scanned in the previous round; none cleared noise.
  • Topology transfer: excellent measured — which is the same evidence read the other way. A model that transfers freely between topologies is not one that is missing topology-specific structure.
Why ensembling could not have worked

The variance decomposition explains it. Infrastructure noise is exactly 0.000 — four runs at one seed are bit-identical. Init-only variance is 0.2–0.5 points. Split variance is 0.6–1.9. Ensemble members must share a split or member B trains on member A's test events, so the construction can only exploit the small term. The premise was real; the usable variance simply lives on the axis this method cannot touch without leaking.

Yes — and this overturns the expected conclusion

Every sample sits far above the 50% coin flip, with the control at exactly 100.0% so the probe is demonstrably working. A GBDT on cluster summaries alone separates the two candidates 84% of the time on Z+jets. The discriminating information is present in reconstructed features, and Deep Sets is not using all of it in its final ordering.

The Deep Sets score is deliberately excluded from the probe's inputs. The pairs are built as (what the model chose, what it should have chosen), so by construction the model scores the wrong one higher — feeding that in would let the probe hit ~100% with the rule "pick whichever the model liked less", which is leakage of the construction rather than physics.

The Z+jets/VBF contrast is structural. VBF's random pairs are far easier than its hard ones (94.9 vs 76.7), so VBF failures are a genuinely confusable minority. Z+jets is nearly flat (85.9 vs 84.2) — its pairs are uniformly hard, consistent with a sample whose forward jets are mostly pileup and where little is cleanly separable.

Perfect jet identity makes Z+jets worse, not better

Restricting WAVeS to truth-matched hard-scatter jets scores 51.4 against Deep Sets' 66.4 — 15 points down, using information no reconstructed tagger could ever have. The per-track truth tagger reaches 79.4 on the same events, so the recoverable information is real; it simply is not organised by jet.

This kills two lines at once. A reco jet HS/PU tagger cannot help if the truth version hurts, and supervising attention on truth jet identity — the hgtdtransformer-style idea of using truth only in the attention so evaluation stays reco-only — would be supervising toward a target measured to be worse than what the model already does unaided.

The mechanism is visible in the control row. Tracks in pileup jets score 54.1, tracks in hard-scatter jets 50.5 — the PU rung is the better of the two. In a sample whose forward jets are overwhelmingly pileup-supplied, "near a hard-scatter forward jet" selects a small, unrepresentative population, while the discriminating tracks are distributed without regard to jets at all. Jet proximity was never carrying the signal, so refining jet identity was never going to.

Splitting the cluster time by jet association

If Z+jets forward jets are mostly pileup, the hard-scatter content should sit outside them. Two uncertainty-weighted times per cluster — in-jet and out-of-jet — plus each one's Δt to truth.

coverage
Only 19.5% of Z+jets clusters have both halves. 78.6% are out-of-jet only — no constituent track inside any jet — and 1.9% in-jet only.

out-of-jet 52.6% vs in-jet 47.4%. The inversion is real: on VBF the in-jet half wins, as it must where the hard scatter is the forward jets.

Real, and small

The predicted inversion is confirmed — but at 52.6/47.4 it is barely a preference, and it applies to a fifth of clusters. The disagreement between the halves is the more useful part: it is a cluster-level inconsistency statistic that does not require choosing a side. Both are exported and are now model inputs; neither is a headline lever.

The vertices we were throwing away

Prompted by re-reading the sample description: RecoVtx_z is a full array, and only index 0 — the primary — was ever read.

The failure gallery found that 74 of 100 wrongly-picked Z+jets clusters have truth purity exactly zero: they are pileup clusters. A pileup cluster is, close to definitionally, one sitting on a reconstructed pileup vertex — and ITk reconstructs those vertices already. That question had never been asked of the data.

The headline number was mostly an artefact — the conditional test is the result

closest_vtx_is_pv separates 82.7% vs 25.0% raw, which looks decisive. Held at fixed |Δz| it collapses to a 1.17–1.46× lift and inverts to 0.84 in the outermost bin. It was restating "this cluster is near the primary" — which delta_z already says and TRKPTZ already exploits. It is exported but deliberately not a model input.

The ranker D justified — and why it lost

A design error, not a refutation of D

The ranker was trained to order pairs by |Δt| — which candidate is closer to truth — while the metric rewards any candidate inside ±60 ps. Those are different objectives, and on Z+jets they diverge most: ordering correctly among candidates that are all outside the window earns nothing. Deep Sets' existing multi-positive listwise loss already puts softmax mass on every qualifying candidate, so it was the metric-aligned objective all along and the ranker replaced it with a worse one.

D is not overturned — 84.2% pairwise separability stands, on pairs built as (chosen, correct). What the result adds is that curated-pair accuracy does not convert into deployment gain by itself, which the earlier stage-2 re-ranker at −0.6 had already hinted. A metric-aligned retry — label by the 60-ps criterion, train only on (good, bad) pairs — remains open, but it is now a backlog item rather than the headline direction.

What the model actually does wrong

100 failing Z+jets events dumped with every candidate, sampled rather than taken in file order.

purity 0
74 of 100 wrongly-picked clusters have truth purity exactly zero — not a marginal HS cluster mis-ordered, but a pure pileup cluster chosen outright.
largest Σ pT
63% of picks are the highest-Σ pT candidate in their event. The model reproduces the Σ pT bias that TRKPTZ and WAVeS are both built on.
reachable
These are recoverable failures: a candidate within 60 ps existed and a different one was chosen.

This is what motivated asking about reconstructed pileup vertices. If the dominant error is picking a pure-pileup cluster, the most direct question available is whether that cluster sits on a vertex ITk already reconstructed — and until now nothing in the feature set could answer it.

Where this goes next

Four lines closed by measurement, one open and in flight.

  1. 1

    Retrain on the vertex-relational features

    In flight. Full re-export running: 7 samples × 2 selections, 152 jobs. The test is whether pv_pu_dz_ratio's 1.78–2.34× lift in the displaced |Δz| bins — measured on local VBF — reproduces on Z+jets, where there is more pileup and the failure mode lives, and whether it converts into core fraction.

    Honest prior: six things have measured null on this gap already. What distinguishes this one is that it targets the specific failure the gallery identified, using information that was demonstrably in the ntuples and demonstrably unread.

  2. 2

    Quantify the half that cannot be closed

    The 13.2-point time-quality term is a result in its own right and is measured at the canonical selection, not inferred. Stated properly — the ML recovers the available selection information to within X, and the remainder is a timing limit of the present reconstruction — it is a stronger claim than any further architecture would produce.

  3. 3

    Metric-aligned pairwise retry

    Backlog. Label by the 60-ps criterion rather than |Δt| ordering, and train only on (good, bad) pairs. Worth doing only if item 1 does not move the number, since Deep Sets' listwise loss is already metric-aligned and the burden is on the pairwise form to beat it.

  4. 4

    Revisit the Z+jets selection itself

    Open question rather than a plan. The failing events are ones where the forward-jet requirement admits an event with almost no hard-scatter forward activity to select on. Requiring ≥1 forward HS track would raise the number by changing the population rather than the algorithm — which is a legitimate result only if stated as such.

Closed by measurement, not abandoned

Jet HS/PU tagger — the truth version scores 15 points below Deep Sets on Z+jets. Truth-jet attention supervision — same measurement; it would supervise toward a worse target. In/out-of-jet split — the inversion is real but worth +2.7 points at a perfect-choice ceiling, on the 19.5% of clusters that have both halves. |Δt|-ordered pairwise ranking — objective mismatch, −4.1. Each was cheap to settle because the ceiling was computed before the model was built.

Two corrections to the plan we started from

The oracle decomposition, proposed as the highest-value first step, was already in hand: the oracle is exactly P(a candidate within 60 ps exists), so Category D is 7.4% and the 25.5 points were always net of it. And the timing-quality variables proposed as missing — σt, timing pulls, timing RMS, χ²t — are all already exported, with per-track time, timeRes, t_pull_cluster and nhgtd_hits already model inputs. Sum-pooling represents Σ1/σ² exactly, so cluster time uncertainty was never outside the architecture's reach.

Infrastructure worth not re-learning

Each of these cost real time to find.

  • The group key is (sample_id, file_idx, event_num). Each export shard restarts event_num at 0, so the old two-column key merged unrelated events into one ranking group once shards were combined — and every row still looked valid.
  • hadd for the exports, hist_merge for the *_hist files. Opposite rules, same reason: TParameter scalars have no Merge(), and the exports have none while the histogram files do.
  • A negative --vbs-deta is a third mode, not a smaller threshold — it drops the VBS pair requirement, which no combination of the two thresholds can express.
  • An unmatchable GPU request idles forever rather than falling back to CPU. The pool has 8 GPU slots; they were not obtainable, and jobs sat idle until the request was removed.
  • Condor is bit-deterministic here. Four runs at one seed agree to every printed digit, so there is no infrastructure noise to budget for — only split and init variance.
