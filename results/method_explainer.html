What the method changes

Five changes, none of which touch the clustering or the track selection. The short version: the answer stops being “the time of the cluster we picked” and becomes a time built from tracks, weighted by how likely each one is to be hard scatter.

What happens today

The baseline, in four steps.

Given an event, the current analysis:

  • selects tracks by z-compatibility with the primary vertex, plus η and pT cuts;
  • groups them into clusters by time agreement (clusterTracksInTime, 3σ iterative);
  • scores each cluster with TRKPTZ and takes the highest;
  • reports that cluster's time as the event t₀.
TRKPTZ = Σᵢ pT,i × exp(−1.5 · |Δz|)

— the summed track pT of the cluster, damped by how far the cluster's z sits from the primary vertex. The time it reports is the precision-weighted mean of that cluster's track times:

t₀ = Σᵢ (tᵢ / σᵢ²) / Σᵢ (1 / σᵢ²)

Both of those are the things I change. The clustering itself, the track selection, and the vertex are untouched.

The five changes

Where each one inserts, and what it replaces.

1 A per-track hard-scatter tagger

A gradient-boosted classifier that answers, for every track: is this track from the hard scatter? Output is a probability pᵢ. It is trained on truth_is_hs and used nowhere as an input feature — only as a weight.

It runs in two rounds. Round 1 uses the track's own row: pT, η, z₀, its z₀ pull to the primary, its time, its timing resolution, HGTD hit count, jet association, and so on. Round 2 adds neighbour context — for each track, the pᵢ-weighted count of other tracks nearby in time and in z, from round 1:

nb_ph60ᵢ = Σ⫺≠ᵢ pᵢʲᵣ · [ |tᵢ − t⫺| < 60 ps ]

The idea is that a hard-scatter track lives among other hard-scatter tracks at the same time and z, and a pileup track does not. Weighting the neighbourhood by round-1 probability stops one confidently-wrong neighbour from poisoning the count. worth +1.7 pts on the Z+jets t₀ alone

2 A learned cluster score, replacing TRKPTZ

Instead of ΣpT·e^(−1.5|Δz|), a classifier predicts directly what we care about: P(this cluster's reported time lands within 60 ps of truth). Its inputs are the cluster's reconstructed summary columns plus the tagger aggregates from ① — how much hard-scatter probability the cluster contains, its pᵢ-weighted time, and so on.

Crucially the training label is the tagw criterion from ②, not the old cluster-time one — the selector is trained on the same quantity the pipeline will actually report. +3.0 to +5.9 pts across samples

3 Re-timing the chosen cluster

Once a cluster is picked, its time is recomputed. Rather than the plain precision-weighted mean, each track is also weighted by its hard-scatter probability:

t_tagw = Σᵢ (pᵢ · tᵢ / σᵢ²) / Σᵢ (pᵢ / σᵢ²)

Then it is Winsorised twice: drop tracks more than 100 ps from that estimate, recompute; drop tracks more than 60 ps from the new estimate, recompute again.

This is the change that needs no new selection at all

It does not alter which cluster was chosen — only what time that cluster reports. It is worth +0.9 to +1.7 points on every sample, which makes it the cheapest change here to adopt: it can be dropped into the existing TRKPTZ pipeline on its own.

4 A second answer, computed from all tracks

The largest conceptual change. Nothing in the ±60 ps criterion requires the answer to be a cluster's time — it only requires a number. So a second estimate is built from every timed track in the event, ignoring cluster boundaries entirely.

A small network emits a weight per track; the weights combine the track times through five unrolled IRLS steps, which converge to a weighted median rather than a mean; and the whole thing trains directly on a smooth version of “is |t₀ − ttruth| < 60 ps”.

Why a median and not a mean

13.7% of genuine hard-scatter tracks sit more than 200 ps from truth, while their quoted resolution is ~25 ps. There is a fat outlier tail the resolution does not describe, and the inverse-variance mean is the estimator most sensitive to it, because it trusts σ. On identical, perfectly-tagged tracks: median 81.7% vs inverse-variance mean 65.3%.

Swapping the estimator inside a cluster does not help (61.9 → 61.6) — the clustering already grouped tracks by time agreement, so within one cluster the times concur. The mis-timed tracks were split off into their own clusters, which is exactly why no single-cluster answer can reach them and why aggregating across the whole event can.

5 A chooser between the two answers

②+③ give one answer; ④ gives another. They fail on different events — their union is 1–3 points above either alone. A final classifier predicts P(the aggregate answer is right and the cluster answer is wrong), trained only on events where exactly one of the two is correct, and switches when that probability crosses a threshold.

Its inputs include corroboration features: how many tracks and clusters sit near each candidate answer, and how far apart the two answers are.

What is not changed

Scoping, so the diff is clear.

  • The clustering. clusterTracksInTime runs exactly as it does now, same 3σ iterative grouping, same DIST_CUT_CONE.
  • Track selection and z-association. Same MAX_NSIGMA cut, same η and pT windows. (The separate z-association study found the incumbent cut is mistuned for TRKPTZ — that finding is untouched and unused here.)
  • The vertex. Primary vertex z is taken as given.
  • The selection / fiducial region. Canonical --vbs-deta=0 --vbs-mjj=500 throughout.
  • The metric. Core fraction at ±60 ps, unchanged, and every number is out-of-fold.

The honest gap to something deployable

A real analysis would train these once on MC and freeze them. That transfer is not measured here. The earlier and much simpler guarded metric was transfer-tested — a VBF-trained model applied frozen to every other sample stayed net-positive everywhere, including three million μ = 0 control events — which is encouraging but is not evidence about this stronger pipeline. Freeze-and-transfer is the first thing I would run next.
